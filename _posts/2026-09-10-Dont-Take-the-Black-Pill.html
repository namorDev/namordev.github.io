I came across this talk by Andrew Kelley, creator of the Zig programming language, at Software Should Work 2026. It is less about programming techniques and more about the responsibility and agency of the people who build software.

In short

Kelley argues against the cynical idea that the future of technology is simply happening to us and that nothing can be done about it. Software often fails its users not because engineers lack the knowledge to build it well, but because business incentives and organizational power point in a different direction.

He calls software that manipulates people coercionware: dark patterns, intrusive advertising, engagement algorithms, unwanted notifications, workplace surveillance, and tools that ultimately serve a company instead of the person using them. His guiding principle is simple: computers should serve the people directly interacting with them.

The hopeful part of the talk is its reminder that developers are not powerless. We can reject fatalism, support free and open-source software, make deliberate ethical choices, and build alternatives that give control back to users. The message I took away: lost ground is not the same as lost hope.
